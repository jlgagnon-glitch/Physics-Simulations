About the model

  • Isometric means static. The arm does not rotate, so the net torque about the shoulder joint is zero and the net force on the arm is zero.
  • Geometry. 2-D front (frontal-plane) view with the elbow straight. Shoulder joint to elbow 30 cm, to wrist 56 cm, to the grip 64 cm. The deltoid heads pull in straight lines from origin to insertion. The supraspinatus wraps over the humeral head (radius 2.4 cm) like a rope over a pulley, so while it wraps its moment arm equals the head's radius.
  • Moment arms change with the angle at the shoulder joint. With these attachments they range from 1.7 to 3.7 cm (middle deltoid), 0 to 3.8 cm (anterior deltoid), and 1.4 to 2.4 cm (supraspinatus). Those ranges are in line with cadaver measurements (for example, Ackland et al., 2008). The anterior deltoid barely abducts with the arm at the side and helps more as the arm rises.
  • Scapulohumeral rhythm. When it is on, arm elevation θ is split between the shoulder joint (glenohumeral angle) and upward rotation of the scapula. The muscles' leverage depends on the glenohumeral angle; the weight's torque depends on θ. For simplicity the scapula rotates about the center of the humeral head.
  • Segments. Upper arm 1.96 kg at 13 cm, forearm 1.10 kg at 41 cm, hand 0.42 kg at 63 cm from the shoulder joint (typical values for a 70 kg adult).
  • How the load is shared. The model picks the combination of forces that keeps muscle activation as low as possible: it minimizes the sum of cubed activations (Crowninshield & Brand, 1981), giving a_i ∝ √(F0i ri). Each muscle's available force F0 = Fmax × fL falls off when its fibers are stretched or shortened away from their optimal length (set at 30° at the shoulder joint). Maximum forces and optimal fiber lengths are approximate values from a widely used upper-limb model (Holzbaur et al., 2005): anterior and middle deltoid 1143 N each (9.8 and 10.8 cm), supraspinatus 488 N (6.8 cm).
  • Left out. The posterior deltoid, the other rotator cuff muscles (which mainly press the humeral head into its socket), and the parts of the forces outside the frontal plane. Tendons are treated as rigid.
  • The given forces are the model's values rounded to the nearest newton, and the answer is calculated from those rounded values, so students' torque balance works out exactly.
